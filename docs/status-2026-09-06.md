## What the evidence says

On 70 held-out matched decision points, the learned outcome-value supervisor
improved mean counterfactual reward by **2.64 points per decision**. It selected
all 7 helpful interventions but also selected 6 harmful interventions. Its
average precision was 0.162 versus 0.100 prevalence and predicted value had a
positive 0.233 correlation with realized reward movement. That is a weak but
real local signal.

The signal failed in complete trajectories. Across 30 paired episodes:

| Arm | Mean reward | Tool calls | Paired mean vs base | Wins / losses / ties |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Base Qwen3.5-9B | -24.06 | 0 | — | — |
| Prior imitation supervisor | -29.78 | 77 | -5.71 | 12 / 7 / 11 |
| Outcome-tuned rule | -36.71 | 63 | -12.65 | 6 / 9 / 15 |
| Learned outcome-value supervisor | -39.82 | 412 | **-15.75** | 12 / 15 / 3 |

The learned arm's paired bootstrap 95% interval was [-55.70, 24.42]. Removing
its largest gain made the mean worse (-22.50), and it caused 12 critical
regressions versus 8 critical improvements. All 412 forced tool calls executed
successfully, so the failure is not retrieval reliability. The local offline
objective does not model the distribution shift and compounding decisions
created by the supervisor inside a full trajectory.

## Decision

**Stop treating the current supervisor as the primary performance lever; do not
begin RL.** A larger replication is not justified merely to make the confidence
interval narrower: the point estimate, outlier check, win/loss balance, and
critical-mistake balance all move in the wrong direction.

The next useful research step is not more SFT on the same labels. If work
continues, build a trajectory-aware intervention target that includes post-tool
state and future policy response, and require a small development-only full-run
canary to beat base before any expensive training. Keep deterministic legality,
tool availability, and budget enforcement outside the learned policy.
